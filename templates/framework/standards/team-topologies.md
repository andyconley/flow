# Team Topologies Standard

This standard defines how team structure and system design interact.

## Core principle

System architecture reflects communication structure. Team design and architecture should not be treated as separate concerns.

## Team types

Useful team shapes include:

- stream-aligned teams
- platform teams
- enabling teams
- complicated-subsystem teams

Projects or organizations should choose deliberately rather than accidentally.

## Interaction modes

Teams usually interact through:

- collaboration
- X-as-a-service
- facilitating

Interaction modes should fit the maturity of the interface and the work.

## Cognitive load limits

When a team owns too much, quality degrades. Reduce scope, simplify ownership, or improve platform support instead of assuming more coordination is free.

## Team-architecture alignment

Team design is an architectural choice:

- platform teams should provide services, not become ticket queues
- enabling teams should transfer capability, not permanently absorb delivery
- complicated-subsystem teams should be used sparingly for truly specialist areas
